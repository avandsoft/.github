# AVANDSOFT — "A DAY WITH SEMICOLON"
### A Video Scenario for Google Vids
**Director's Cut — Life-Integration Film**
**Runtime: 90 seconds | Format: 16:9, 1080p | Tone: Warm, real, observational — like a day in someone's life, not a tech ad**

---

## DIRECTOR'S NOTE

Last cut was a mood piece. This one is a *life*. We follow one person — **MAYA, early 30s** — through an ordinary Tuesday where three completely different decisions show up, the way they actually do: not in a boardroom, but at a kitchen counter, on a train, on the couch at 11pm. Semicolon isn't a hero swooping in; it's the friend in her pocket she checks in with before she commits. We shoot handheld-adjacent, natural light, real locations — this should feel like a Google Pixel "Day in the Life" film, not a SaaS explainer. The app appears only as much as a phone naturally appears in someone's day: quick glances, not lingering UI tours.

Color palette: naturalistic — warm morning light, cool overcast midday, golden lamp-light evening. No sci-fi blue glow. The only stylization: every time Maya makes a decision, we get a half-second freeze-frame with a soft semicolon watermark in the corner — subtle, like a punctuation mark closing a thought.

---

## SCENE 1 — "THE APARTMENT" (0:00–0:16)
**Visual:** 7:40am. Maya's kitchen, morning light through a window. She's making coffee one-handed, phone propped against the toaster. On screen (glimpsed, not zoomed): two apartment listings side by side — one cheaper but far from work, one pricier but a 10-minute walk. She's chewing her lip, scrolling between them.
**Action beat:** She opens Semicolon, taps "New Decision Scenario," types the two options in about three seconds — we see her thumbs move, not the keyboard in detail. The app auto-populates factors: *Commute time, Monthly cost, Neighborhood safety, Lease length.*
**Camera:** Handheld, over-the-shoulder and side profile, natural kitchen sounds (kettle, spoon on mug).
**Sound design:** Ambient kitchen — coffee maker gurgle, a dog barking outside, no music yet.
**On-screen text:** *(minimal — just a glimpse of the app's factor grid filling in)*
**VO (Maya, warm, a little tired, talking almost to herself / friend on the phone tone):** "I've been going back and forth on this apartment thing for a week."
**Beat:** She glances at the recommendation that appears — a simple highlighted card. She exhales, half-smiles, grabs her coffee.
**Maya (to her roommate, off-screen, casually):** "Okay — I think I'm taking the one on Elm."

*(Half-second freeze-frame, soft semicolon watermark, then hard cut.)*

## SCENE 2 — "THE TRAIN" (0:16–0:34)
**Visual:** 8:15am, on a commuter train, gray morning outside the window. Maya's seated, phone in hand, half-listening to the train announcement. A coworker, **DEV**, sits beside her, glancing at her screen.
**Dev:** "You still holding that crypto or did you finally sell?"
**Maya (small laugh):** "I don't guess anymore." She turns the phone slightly so Dev can see — Semicolon's Investment Insights screen: a simple chart, a "Hold" indicator glowing steady, a short line of reasoning beneath it ("Volatility elevated, medium-term trend stable").
**Camera:** Two-shot on the train, natural rocking motion, window light flickering across their faces as the train passes stations.
**Sound design:** Train ambience — rails, muffled announcement, quiet.
**Dev:** "Huh. That's oddly reassuring."
**Maya:** "Right? It's not telling me what to do. It's just... showing me why."
**On-screen text:** *(brief, small, unobtrusive)* "Buy. Hold. Reconsider — with the data behind it."

*(Freeze-frame, semicolon watermark, cut.)*

---

## SCENE 3 — "THE LUNCH BREAK" (0:34–0:50)
**Visual:** 12:30pm, Maya at a small table outside a café, sandwich half-eaten, sun a bit stronger now. She's got five minutes and pulls up a Semicolon questionnaire notification — "New questionnaire available."
**Action beat:** She taps through a few quick questions (we see 2-3 real, relatable ones flash by — quick, humanizing, not clinical): *"When facing a tough choice, do you usually decide fast or take your time?"* She smiles slightly answering — it's a little like a personality quiz, low-stakes, a bit fun.
**Camera:** Close on her face reacting to a question that makes her chuckle, then a wider shot of the café patio, life happening around her.
**Sound design:** Café ambience — espresso machine hiss, distant chatter, birds.
**On-screen text:** A small golden token icon ("+12 Doxa") pops gently in the corner as she finishes — understated, like a notification, not a fireworks moment.
**VO (Maya, lighter, amused):** "Turns out answering a few questions about how I think... actually pays off."

*(Freeze-frame, semicolon watermark, cut.)*

---

## SCENE 4 — "THE COUCH, 11:04PM" (0:50–1:12)
**Visual:** Late evening, lamp-lit living room, Maya in a hoodie, feet up, laptop balanced on her knees next to her phone. This is the "big one" — a real, weightier decision: a job offer. On screen we briefly see two offer cards: *Current role (stable, familiar)* vs. *New offer (higher pay, more risk, relocation).*
**Action beat:** She opens Semicolon, starts a new Decision Scenario. This time she pauses before entering factors — genuinely thinking, not performing for camera. A small prompt appears: *"You have enough Doxa to unlock a deeper analysis. Use them?"* She taps yes without much hesitation — we sense she's used this before and trusts it.
**Camera:** Slow, quiet push-in on her face, dim lamp light, the blue glow of the screen the only cool light in an otherwise warm room.
**Sound design:** Near silence — a clock ticking softly, the faint sound of a night outside (a car passing, wind). No music until the very end of this scene.
**Beat:** The app lays out a clean comparison — risk factors, financial impact, career trajectory — nothing overwhelming, just organized. Maya reads it slowly, really reads it, then sets the phone face-down on her chest and stares at the ceiling for a beat. Not because the app told her what to do — because now she actually *can* think clearly.
**VO (Maya, quieter, more honest than earlier in the day):** "It doesn't make the decision for me. It just... clears the fog enough that I can."
**Beat:** She picks the phone back up, taps "Accept new offer" (implied, not shown explicitly) — small, resolved nod to herself.

*(Freeze-frame, semicolon watermark — held a beat longer this time.)*

## SCENE 5 — "THE SIGN-OFF" (1:12–1:24)
**Visual:** Cut to black. The semicolon mark draws itself in light, same as before, but now it feels earned rather than abstract — we've seen what it means all day. It settles, then the logo lockup resolves beneath it: **Semicolon**, then **by Avandsoft**.
**Camera:** Static, centered, calm.
**Color grade:** Warm black, soft glow, no cold sci-fi tone — consistent with the day we just lived in.
**Sound design:** A single warm piano or acoustic note, understated — the score that's been quietly present all film finally resolves here.
**On-screen text:**
- "Every day has a few pauses worth getting right."
- "Semicolon — decide with clarity."
- [Website / App Store / Download CTA]
**VO (Maya, final line, simple and unforced):** "It's not a big deal every time. It's just... a little clearer, every time."

## PRODUCTION NOTES FOR GOOGLE VIDS

- **Casting/talent note:** If using stock or AI-generated actors in Google Vids, choose a consistent "Maya" across all scenes (same face/wardrobe continuity) — the emotional throughline depends on it being one recognizable person's day, not four different stock actors.
- **Locations:** Kitchen (morning), train interior, café patio (midday), living room with lamp (night) — all should read as *lived-in*, not staged corporate stock footage. Slight clutter, real mugs, real light.
- **App UI glimpses:** Keep every on-screen app moment under 2 seconds per shot. The product should feel like a natural part of her day, not a demo. Resist the urge to zoom in and explain features — let Dev's line in Scene 2 and Maya's VO do the explaining instead.
- **Sound:** No music at all until Scene 4's final beat and Scene 5 — silence and ambient sound carry the realism. This is a deliberate contrast to typical tech ads.
- **Pacing:** Scenes 1–3 are quick, light, almost breezy (16–18 sec each). Scene 4 slows down noticeably — give it room to breathe; this is the emotional core.
- **Alt shorter cut (30 sec):** Use Scene 1 (trimmed to 8 sec), Scene 4 (trimmed to 15 sec), and Scene 5 in full.
- **Alt vertical cut (9:16 for social):** Reframe Scenes 1, 3, and 4 only — these read best in close-up/vertical without losing story clarity.
